**The idea.** StudyZen came from a problem I live with: my study material is scattered. Notes are in one app, questions to an AI in another, a calculator and dictionary in two more, and nothing tells me what I should study today. StudyZen puts one subject, or Study Space, in one place. It builds a plan and a learning path, and inside each lesson the Ask AI, Scan, Dictionary, Calculator, Research and Notes tools remember their history, so I can reopen yesterday's chat instead of starting again. Quizzes, session timers and progress show whether the plan is working.

I made honesty a feature. AI is bring-your-own-key and the key never leaves the device. Wikipedia results are real retrieved text, YouTube and web results are plainly search links, and anything a model wrote is labelled AI-generated. A judge can also try the whole app in guest mode without an account.

**Technologies.** Kotlin Multiplatform with Compose Multiplatform lets one codebase, about 94% shared, run on Android, Desktop, Web and iOS. Ktor handles networking, kotlinx.serialization the data, and platform-specific code is limited to small expect/actual classes and Android features such as alarm reminders and the camera. The backend uses Node.js, Express, MongoDB Atlas, Gmail SMTP and Firebase Admin for accounts, e-mail verification, sync and push messages.
